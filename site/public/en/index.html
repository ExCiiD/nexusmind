01 — Solo training

Everything that happens between two games.

Ascend runs in the background while you play. Between two games, it gives you what you need to understand what happened, and what to work on in the next one.

  1. 01Recording

    Your games record themselves.

    Launch your game: Ascend captures the video and the match timeline. Cut a clip with two handles, then keep it, or share it on YouTube, Discord or by link. If you turn it on, every game goes to your YouTube channel by itself.

  2. 02Micro-review

    Three moments, forty-five seconds.

    Right after the game, Ascend shows you the three moments that cost you the most, in your VOD’s player. One click per moment to say what happened, or “Not a mistake” when it got it wrong.

  3. 03Post-game review

    Your VOD, annotated.

    One player, with the micro-review beside it: filter the timeline’s events, take a note (N), tag a mistake (E) to the second, and correct the mistakes Ascend detected.

  4. 04Statistics

    What makes you win, in numbers.

    CS, vision, trades, objectives: your stats compared with your own averages, to see what really changes when you win.

  5. 05Mistake Lab

    Your recurring mistakes, sorted.

    Every tagged mistake joins the lab, and you correct the ones Ascend got wrong. You see which ones come back, what they cost, and which one to tackle first.

02 — Team environment

Staff and players on the same tool.

Coaching, management, scheduling and team analysis in one place. Each player keeps their solo training; the staff sees what the players choose to share.

  • Schedule

    The team’s week, without a spreadsheet.

    Scrims, reviews and 1:1s in one calendar, with the slots when every starter is free.

  • Team dashboard

    The whole team on one page.

    Each player’s form, playing volume and alerts, with what deserves a talk this week.

  • Collective stats

    Scrims and tournaments, in numbers.

Also: draft simulator, champion pools, scouting, LFT posts, weekly report, trial players.

03 — Questions

Frequently asked questions

Another question? Write to us: exciid501@gmail.com

Is Ascend free?

Yes. Every feature described here is free during the beta, and a complete free version will always remain available.

How do I get access?

The beta is invite-only. A member can create a single-use code, valid for 14 days. The code of a team or coaching group that invites you also opens access.

Is it allowed by Riot Games?

Ascend uses Riot Games’ official public API and follows its policies: no game modification, no information during the game, no live advantage. Ascend isn’t endorsed by Riot Games (see the notice at the bottom of the page).

Does it work on Mac?

The desktop application is available for Windows 10 and 11. The web app (reviews, coaching, schedule, scrims) works on every device but does not record games.

Does recording slow the game down?

Capture uses your graphics card’s hardware encoder when available. Quality, frame rate and folder are configurable, and automatic recording can be turned off.

Do my teammates see my statistics?

Only if you decide so. Sharing is set group by group (reviews, statistics, team data, wellbeing) and can be withdrawn at any time.

Is there AI?

Not at the core of the application: the micro-review and the assistant apply coaching rules written in advance, which you can read. Voice transcription of debriefs uses a model that runs on your PC; nothing is sent to an AI service.

What does the engine know about the game?

Only what Riot’s official match timeline proves, sampled every minute: positions, gold, XP, levels, CS, kills, objectives, towers, wards and purchases. Ascend applies coaching rules written in advance to it: the map (lanes, rivers, pits, towers), objective timers, economy thresholds, the level race, vision, tempo and role-specific patterns (46 in all). A win-chance estimate, drawn from gold, XP and objectives, only serves to set aside moments when the game was already decided: it is never shown as a rating. What the data does not contain (spells cast, minion waves, where a ward was placed), the engine does not guess.

How does the Mistake Lab spot my mistakes?

Three ways. 16 mistakes are spotted without you on the timeline, by fixed rules: dying with more than 1,500 gold in your pocket, for instance, or being 15 CS behind your lane opponent at 10:00. The others come from your micro-review answers (a “justified” answer never counts) and from your own tags during a review. A mistake already recorded is never counted twice. The Lab then ranks each mistake by frequency, by moment of the game and by the win-rate gap between games with and without it, and offers the Codex entry that fixes it.

Can the micro-review be wrong?

It suggests, you decide. It only sees the match timeline, sampled every minute by Riot: when the data proves nothing, it stays silent. And every moment keeps its “justified” way out.
