Deliberate practice

Players who improve play with an objective, review themselves and measure. Ascend takes care of what happens between two games, where most people give up.

Calibration flash

Every 15 games, a single question: which fundamental cost you the most? Ascend then shows the gap between your answer and what your micro-reviews say.

2

The micro-review in 45 seconds

A full review takes twenty minutes, and most players never do one. The micro-review only shows what weighed on the result, and ends before the next queue.

At most 3 moments
The costliest of the game, at least 25 seconds apart. When there is none, Ascend says so and lets you queue again.
Three triggers
A death followed, within 90 seconds, by a lost objective or a clear drop in your chances. A major objective taken by the enemy while you were alive, with no ward of yours and nothing taken elsewhere. A deficit at 10:00: more than 15 CS or 400 XP behind your lane opponent.
41 recognised patterns
A gank on a predictable 2:30 timer, a desynchronised recall, a shutdown given first in a fight, a mid roam paid in plates, a support inside the pit instead of the choke point.
Garbage time does not count
When the game was already decided at the time of the action, above 85% or under 18% from gold, XP and objectives, the moment is dropped: it would teach you nothing. One exception above 85%: a jungler who recalls during Baron or forces a dive after an inhibitor, two ways to throw a won game.
A deliberate call is not a mistake
Every question keeps a “justified” way out. It is recorded, never counted against you.
No AI, nothing sent
Rules written in advance, run on your PC, with no connection. The same game always gives the same moments.
75 Codex entries
After your answer, the Codex gives the tactical rule and a precise training goal. A few entries show the sequence in an official professional match.

3

The Mistake Lab

Every tagged mistake lands here, whether it comes from you, from the micro-review or from the automatic detector. The Lab answers three questions: which one comes back most, how much it costs you, and when in the game it happens.

  • 26mistakes in the catalogue, in four families
  • 15spotted without you, on the official match timeline
  • 2 gamesat least on each side before a win-rate gap is shown

The gap reads in win-rate points: the games where the mistake happens against the other analysed games. A mistake can become the objective of your next sessions; Ascend tracks it and tells you whether it recedes.

4

The fundamentals fingerprint

Nine axes, two windows: your last 15 games and your last 40. The gap between the two shapes is your current form.

Supports get their own axes: vision, fight presence, crowd control, and a fight impact that adds up heals, shields, damage dealt and damage absorbed. An enchanter, a poke mage and an engage support are each judged on their own job. Farm says nothing about their game.

The fingerprint completes a self-assessment of 38 fundamentals, in 7 families. When your numbers drift from what you declared, Ascend suggests doing it again.

What Ascend does with Riot data

Ascend relies on the official Riot Games API, read-only, and follows Riot’s developer policies. Here is exactly what the application requests, and what it never does.

After the game, never during
Ascend shows nothing in game: no overlay, no information about opponents, no live advantage. The game’s local Live Client Data API is only used to know when a game starts and ends, to start and stop recording.
The key stays on our servers
The application ships no API key. Requests go through an Ascend relay (HTTPS) that adds the key, only allows a closed list of read-only calls and rate-limits each account.
Match data is cached
Each match is requested once, then kept on your PC. This respects Riot’s rate limits and keeps the application fast.
No home-made ranking
Ascend does not estimate MMR and does not replace the official ladder. The estimate of your winning chances is only used to drop garbage time after the game; it is never shown as a rating.
What you choose to share
Your coaching data stays private. A coach or teammate only sees what you agreed to share with their group, and you can withdraw that at any time.
Public data only
Scouting only uses the public match history of the Riot IDs you enter. Ascend does not try to identify anonymous players.

Questions

Is Ascend free?

Yes. Every feature described here is free during the beta, and a complete free version will always remain available.

How do I get access?

The beta is invite-only. A member can create a single-use code, valid for 14 days. The code of a team or coaching group that invites you also opens access.

Is it allowed by Riot Games?

Ascend uses Riot Games’ official public API and follows its policies: no game modification, no information during the game, no live advantage. Ascend isn’t endorsed by Riot Games (see the notice at the bottom of the page).

Does it work on Mac?

The desktop application is available for Windows 10 and 11. The web app (reviews, coaching, schedule, scrims) works on every device but does not record games.

Does recording slow the game down?

Capture uses your graphics card’s hardware encoder when available. Quality, frame rate and folder are configurable, and automatic recording can be turned off.

Do my teammates see my statistics?

Only if you decide so. Sharing is set group by group (reviews, statistics, team data, wellbeing) and can be withdrawn at any time.

Is there AI?

Not at the core of the application: the micro-review and the assistant apply coaching rules written in advance, which you can read. Voice transcription of debriefs uses a model that runs on your PC; nothing is sent to an AI service.

What does the engine know about the game?

Only what Riot’s official match timeline proves, sampled every minute: positions, gold, XP, levels, CS, kills, objectives, towers, wards and purchases. Ascend applies coaching rules written in advance to it: the map (lanes, rivers, pits, towers), objective timers, economy thresholds, the level race, vision, tempo and role-specific patterns (41 in all). A win-chance estimate, drawn from gold, XP and objectives, only serves to set aside moments when the game was already decided: it is never shown as a rating. What the data does not contain (spells cast, minion waves, where a ward was placed), the engine does not guess.

How does the Mistake Lab spot my mistakes?

Three ways. 15 mistakes are spotted without you on the timeline, by fixed rules: dying with more than 1,500 gold in your pocket, for instance, or being 15 CS behind your lane opponent at 10:00. The others come from your micro-review answers (a “justified” answer never counts) and from your own tags during a review. A mistake already recorded is never counted twice. The Lab then ranks each mistake by frequency, by moment of the game and by the win-rate gap between games with and without it, and offers the Codex entry that fixes it.

Can the micro-review be wrong?

It suggests, you decide. It only sees the match timeline, sampled every minute by Riot: when the data proves nothing, it stays silent. And every moment keeps its “justified” way out.
