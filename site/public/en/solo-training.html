Solo training

Every game becomes a training session.

Players who improve play with an objective, review themselves and measure. Ascend takes care of everything that happens between two games: the objective before, the recording during, the review right after, and the numbers that tell you whether it works.

01 — The method

One game, four moves.

What makes you improve happens between two games, where people usually give up. Ascend shrinks each move to a few seconds.

  1. 01

    Choose an objective

    Ascend suggests 1 to 3 fundamentals out of 38, based on your recent games and your mistakes. You accept, or pick your own.

    30 s

  2. 02

    Play

    The game is detected and recorded. Nothing shows up while you play.

    the game

  3. 03

    Review

    At most 3 critical moments, the VOD cued 12 seconds before each. One click per moment.

    45 s

  4. 04

    Measure

    The objective is checked for you, and your last 15 games are compared with your last 40.

    10 s

02 — In the app

What you do between two games.

Every screen below comes from the application, with a demonstration account.

  1. 01Objective sessions

    Play with an objective, not on autopilot.

    Before you queue, pick 1 to 3 fundamentals to work on. Ascend follows them game after game and gives a verdict at the end of the session.

    • 38 fundamentals, suggested from your recent games and your mistakes
    • Objective checked automatically after every game
    • A flash calibration every 15 games: which fundamental cost you the most?
    • My plan: your current objectives, and the ones your coach assigns you

  2. 02Recording

    Every game filmed, hands off.

    Recording starts and stops with the game. Each video is linked to its match: champion, KDA, result, duration.

    • Built-in capture, or your OBS, Outplayed and Insight folders
    • A library sorted by day, filters by queue, result and review
    • Clips: in and out points, mp4 export
    • Videos stay on your PC

  3. 03Micro-review

    Three moments. Forty-five seconds.

    A full review takes twenty minutes, and most players never do one. The micro-review only shows what weighed on the result, and ends before the next queue.

    • At most 3 moments, at least 25 seconds apart
    • 41 recognised patterns: gank on a timer, desynced recall, first shutdown given away…
    • Garbage time is set aside: it would teach you nothing
    • A “justified” way out: a decision you stand by is not a mistake
    • 109 Codex entries, each with a precise training goal

  4. 04Post-game review

    Your VOD, annotated to the second.

    For the games that deserve more than 45 seconds: the video, the match timeline and your notes in one place.

    • Tag a mistake (X key) with its severity and a note
    • Bookmarks (B) and timestamped notes
    • Mistakes detected automatically on the match timeline
    • Laning phase 0–14 min compared with your lane opponent

  5. 05Statistics

    What makes you win, in numbers.

    Your post-game stats compared with your own averages, to see what really changes when you win.

    • The details of every game, and your values at 15 minutes
    • Your trends over your recent games
    • What sets your wins apart from your losses
    • Your official ranking, as Riot provides it

  6. 06Mistake Lab

    Your recurring mistakes, sorted by what they cost.

    Every tagged mistake lands here, whether it comes from you, the micro-review or the automatic detector. Which one comes back the most, how much it costs you, when it happens.

    • 27 mistakes in the catalogue, in four families
    • 16 spotted without you, on the official match timeline
    • Occurrences, share of the total, win-rate gap
    • A mistake can become your objective; Ascend tells you whether it goes down

  7. 07Fundamentals fingerprint

    Your current form, on a radar.

    Nine axes, two windows: your last 15 games and your last 40. The gap between the two shapes is your current form.

    • Axes dedicated to supports: vision, presence, crowd control
    • A self-assessment of 38 fundamentals, in 7 families
    • Fixed scales: the fingerprint compares you with no one

04 — Privacy

Your data stays with you.

Ascend runs on your PC first. The account is for backups, syncing your devices and working with your coach or team.

After the game, never during
Nothing shows up in game: no overlay, no information about opponents, no live advantage.
Videos never uploaded
Recordings stay on your disk. You choose what you publish.
Local first
Sessions, reviews, notes and statistics live in the application, which works offline.
No AI at the core
The micro-review applies rules written in advance, on your PC, offline.
What Ascend does with Riot data

Ascend relies on the official Riot Games API, read-only, and follows Riot’s developer policies. Here is exactly what the application requests, and what it never does.

After the game, never during
Ascend shows nothing in game: no overlay, no information about opponents, no live advantage. The game’s local Live Client Data API is only used to know when a game starts and ends, to start and stop recording.
The key stays on our servers
The application ships no API key. Requests go through an Ascend relay (HTTPS) that adds the key, only allows a closed list of read-only calls and rate-limits each account.
Match data is cached
Each match is requested once, then kept on your PC. This respects Riot’s rate limits and keeps the application fast.
No home-made ranking
Ascend does not estimate MMR and does not replace the official ladder. The estimate of your winning chances is only used to drop garbage time after the game; it is never shown as a rating.
What you choose to share
Your coaching data stays private. A coach or teammate only sees what you agreed to share with their group, and you can withdraw that at any time.
Public data only
Scouting only uses the public match history of the Riot IDs you enter. Ascend does not try to identify anonymous players.
